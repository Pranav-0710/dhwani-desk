# Dhwani Desk: write-up

A Kannada voice helpline for civic complaints, built for the Great Indian AI Internship Challenge. A caller speaks Kannada over a phone-quality line, and the agent files a ticket or, when someone may be in danger, hands the call to an officer. Gnani Prisma listens, Gnani Timbre speaks, and a fine-tuned open model (Laya) makes every decision on a laptop CPU. There is no LLM in the call.

- Repo: https://github.com/Pranav-0710/dhwani-desk
- Demo video: [LinkedIn](https://lnkd.in/p/gNMNPkh4), [X](https://x.com/jey_pranav53258/status/2116888213851963672)
- Teaser: [X](https://x.com/jey_pranav53258/status/2116867959910191274)

The city in the demo, "Nagara Seva, City Corporation of Hosapura", is fictional, and all data is synthetic.

## The problem

Civic helplines in Kannada-speaking cities are built around English menus, office hours and one operator at a time. Urgent calls (an open manhole, a live wire on a flooded road) wait behind routine ones, and complaints reach the wrong department. Dhwani Desk lets a caller just talk, in Kannada, on an ordinary phone line, and puts urgent calls in front of a person at once.

## What it does

1. The caller says what is wrong. The agent asks only for what is missing (the problem, the ward, a landmark), reads back the department and ward, and gives a ticket number.
2. If the caller describes a danger (a fallen live wire, a child near an open drain) or asks for a person, the agent says a fixed safety line and hands the call to an officer straight away, before any routine question.
3. The supervisor console shows every live call: the transcript, each decision with its confidence, safety alerts, and the tickets. An officer can take over a call and type replies, which Timbre speaks in the agent's voice, then hand it back.
4. The caller can interrupt the agent (barge-in), as on a real call.

## Models and APIs used

| Part | What we use | Where it runs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Speech to text | Gnani **Prisma v2.5** realtime, `kn-IN`, 8 kHz telephony audio | Gnani API |
| Text to speech | Gnani **Timbre v2.5**, `kn-IN`, voice Saanvi, streamed | Gnani API |
| Decisions | **Laya** 0.4.6 multilingual decision engine (`convaiinnovations/laya-multilingual`), fine-tuned on synthetic Kannada complaints, exported to ONNX | Laptop CPU (ONNX Runtime) |
| Training data | **Qwen3-14B** (4-bit) writes the synthetic caller sentences and checks their meaning | Kaggle, 2×T4 |
| Fine-tuning | Laya's own fine-tuning code, 2 epochs for the live checkpoint | Kaggle, 2×T4 |

The fine-tuned checkpoint and the datasets are in private Hugging Face repos (`Dhwanidesk/dhwani-desk-laya` at tag `kn-ft-v1`, `Dhwanidesk/dhwani-desk-data`). The backend pins the tag, and every ticket stores the checkpoint revision that decided it.

## How it works

One FastAPI process sits between the caller and Gnani:

- The browser phone page captures the mic, downsamples to 8 kHz and can add phone-line noise. Audio streams over a WebSocket to the backend, which relays it to Prisma.
- Prisma's transcript segments are joined into caller turns. Each turn goes to Laya in two steps: first four safety questions (a hazard, how urgent, wants a person, out of scope), then the rest (department, ward, whether a location was given, repeat complaint, caller's state, a personal ID number). A safety risk is acted on before the second step runs; if the second step is slow, the agent says "one moment".
- A deterministic state machine picks the next reply from Kannada templates. There is no generated text in the call. Fixed lines are cached after their first synthesis, so they play without waiting for Timbre.
- Tickets go to SQLite with the department, ward, urgency, the caller's landmark verbatim, every decision's probability and the checkpoint revision. Digits that look like a personal ID are masked before storage.

Design and decisions: [`docs/PRD.md`](PRD.md), [`docs/decisions/`](decisions/).

## Data

| Set | Size | How it was made |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seed | 50 | Hand-written and hand-labelled caller turns, used for the week-1 benchmark |
| Training | 2,874 | Generated by Qwen3-14B from sampled scenarios. A row is kept only if it passes text checks and the model, shown the sentence without the request, picks the scenario it was written for (931 attempts failed this and were rewritten) |
| Held-out test | 150 | Written separately and checked by hand, never seen by the generator or training. Spoken by four Timbre voices at three speeds, then transcribed by Prisma at 16 kHz and at 8 kHz with line noise |

A 10% hand check of the training set (289 rows) found the labels right for the sentence in 97% of rows, but only 28% of the sentences were natural Kannada a caller could say: the generator often uses wrong words or garbled clauses. So the model learns from noisy text, and the held-out test set measures what that costs. The checks were made against the labelling rules, not by a native Kannada speaker.

## Results

Held-out test set, spoken, Prisma at 16 kHz ([`eval/reports/test-kn-ft-v1-timbre.md`](../eval/reports/test-kn-ft-v1-timbre.md)). A department counts only when the agent would act on it (confidence ≥ 0.60).

| Metric | Target | Keyword rules | Laya zero-shot | **Laya fine-tuned** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Department macro-F1 | ≥ 0.80 | 0.68 | 0.13 | **0.71** |
| Safety hazard recall | ≥ 0.90 | 0.41 | 0.00 | **0.88** (precision 0.58) |
| Wants a human, recall | ≥ 0.90 | 0.43 | 0.14 | **0.43** |
| Department macro-F1 drop, 8 kHz + noise | reported | 0.01 | 0.00 | **0.01** |

- Fine-tuning made Laya usable for Kannada: zero-shot it could not route a call (0.13) or spot a hazard (0.00).
- It misses all three accuracy targets. Safety recall is close (30 of 34 hazards caught), but it over-flags: ordinary drain and pothole complaints are often treated as hazards, so they reach an officer instead of getting a ticket. We prefer that error to a missed emergency, but it costs officer time.
- "Wants a human" is weak (3 of 7). There are few such rows in the training data, and the hand check found some mislabelled.
- Phone-line audio costs almost nothing: the 8 kHz noisy line drops macro-F1 by about 0.01.
- The test audio is synthetic speech, so these numbers measure the line and Prisma, not real callers' voices.

**A second fine-tune did not replace the first.** To fix the two weakest results, we generated new data with more callers asking for a person and more everyday drain and road complaints, then filtered it after a hand check. We wrote the rule for switching checkpoints before scoring: no fall in safety recall, and a real gain somewhere ([`eval/compare.py`](../eval/compare.py)). `kn-ft-v2` met the gain but broke the safety rule ([`eval/reports/switch-kn-ft-v2.md`](../eval/reports/switch-kn-ft-v2.md)), so `kn-ft-v1` stays live.

| Spoken, 16 kHz | `kn-ft-v1` (live) | `kn-ft-v2` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wants a human, recall | 3 of 7 | **5 of 7** |
| Urgency accuracy | 0.49 | **0.57** |
| Department macro-F1 | 0.71 | 0.72 |
| Safety hazard recall | **30 of 34** | 28 of 34 |
| Safety hazard precision | **0.58** | 0.54 |
| Caller state accuracy | **0.72** | 0.65 |

On the noisy 8 kHz line, v2 caught 26 of 34 hazards against v1's 29. The fix for false hazards did not work, and it cost real ones. The test set is small, so a sentence or two can move these scores, but a missed emergency is the error we least accept. We did not tune v2 further against this test set, because that would stop it being a fair test.

**Latency** ([`docs/decisions/laya-latency.md`](decisions/laya-latency.md)): the agent says "ಸರಿ" ("okay") as soon as the caller finishes, about 1.5 s after their last word (the target is 1.5 s). The answer itself follows 3.7 to 6 s after the caller stopped on a busy laptop, about 2.8 s on a quieter run: Prisma's transcript (about 0.75 s), a 0.6 s pause to let the caller finish, Laya (1 to 4 s depending on load, mostly the safety step) and Timbre (about 0.3 s). On the first turn of a call, Prisma's first transcript sometimes took about 12 s.

## What we would do next

- Train on better Kannada: have native speakers write or fix the training sentences. Generating a new set did not fix this: in our hand check, by a non-native reader, only 35% of the second set's sentences read as natural. The officer-request gain in `kn-ft-v2` is worth keeping, so the next run should add those rows without losing hazard recall.
- Make the safety step faster (int8, once it keeps the fine-tuned model's answers), so the answer follows the acknowledgement sooner.
- Test with real voices reading the test set, and on a real phone line (LiveKit).

## Privacy

Synthetic data only: no real names, phone numbers or ID numbers. Decisions run locally, so only audio goes to Prisma and only reply text goes to Timbre. Personal ID digits are masked before storage, and every decision is stored with its probability and model version for audit.
